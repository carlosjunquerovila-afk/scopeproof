# ScopeProof — a specific commercial test

**Planning document, not sales evidence.** No outreach, customer interview, paid pilot, invoice or revenue was performed by this package. Amounts below are proposed experiment prices, not market findings or a demonstrated willingness to pay.

## Who and what

**First segment:** owner-led web studios delivering fixed-price brochure websites or landing pages, with a clearly defined client reviewer and milestone acceptance criteria.

**Initial offer:** a guided “milestone handoff pilot” covering one active project and up to three milestone reviews. Define the scope with the studio, structure a textual acceptance report, let the studio review the findings, and measure the handoff. Keep invoicing in the studio's existing system during validation; the current prototype's Sandbox adapter is not a production billing service.

**Value proposition to test:** “Make the evidence, acceptance and invoice preparation refer to the same version of the work.” A shorter review and fewer corrections would be desirable results, not claims to make before measurement.

## Proposed experiment

1. Build a manually verified list of ten eligible studios. Do not contact them until outreach is explicitly authorized. Record source, fit and named role; avoid indiscriminate scraping or invented contacts.
2. Ask about the last completed milestone: who approved it, how acceptance was recorded, what changed later, and how much staff time went into resolving ambiguity. Ask for concrete recent behavior before pitching.
3. Seek three voluntary supervised usability sessions with sanitized reports or synthetic substitutes. Avoid client-confidential documents in external models without the studio's informed authorization.
4. If the workflow is useful, offer two paid pilots at a proposed fixed **€99 each**, with a clear deliverable: configure one project, guide up to three reviews and deliver an audit export plus an outcome summary. This is a concierge service experiment, not a SaaS subscription or promise to eliminate disputes.
5. Deliver the agreed service before claiming success. Obtain explicit permission before publishing any quote, logo or case study.

## Predefined success and stop rules

| Question | Measure | Decision rule for this small pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Is the problem frequent enough? | Specific recent handoff problems described by the ten qualified prospects. | Continue discovery if at least four give a concrete case; otherwise narrow or change the segment. |
| Does the workflow reduce effort? | Paired task time for the studio's existing process versus ScopeProof, including setup and correction time. | Investigate continuation if at least two of three sessions save time and no critical acceptance error occurs. Do not generalize from three people. |
| Will anyone pay? | Two independent purchases at the stated pilot price, collected through an authorized live billing method. | Two actual payments support a limited willingness-to-pay finding; verbal interest does not. |
| Can it be delivered economically? | Actual support time, model cost, software cost, payment fees and refund costs. | Redesign if the pilot's direct contribution is negative; do not hide founder/agent labor. |
| Are users misled? | Participant explanation of AI status, identity limits and draft-versus-payment status. | Pause the pilot if users mistake the tool's output for verified delivery or an authenticated client's signature. |

The thresholds are design choices for an early learning exercise, not statistical validation. Record failed offers and failed sessions as well as successful ones.

## Economics to measure, not assume

At €99, a purely illustrative scenario of 90 minutes of delivery at a €30/hour labor valuation, €3 in model/operating costs and €4 in payment/other direct fees leaves €47 before acquisition, overhead, taxes and refunds. None of those cost assumptions is observed. If actual delivery takes three hours at the same labor valuation, the same illustrative contribution becomes €2. This makes delivery time a critical falsification metric.

Do not present the €198 from two proposed pilots as revenue until payment is real and any delivery/refund obligations are understood. No subscription price should be promoted before repeat usage and support costs are measured.

## Interview and session prompts

- “Walk me through the last milestone your client accepted. What exact evidence and version did they approve?”
- “Show me where you recorded that acceptance. What happened if the deliverable changed afterwards?”
- “Which part took the most staff time? What was the consequence?”
- During a task: “What does this finding prove? Who is allowed to approve? Has anything been invoiced, sent or paid?”
- After the task: “What would make this worth paying for? What would prevent you from using it next week?”

Ask for observed behavior, not compliments. Distinguish a product defect, a missing capability and a customer who has no need for the product.

## Scope limits for the pilot

Sell only a supervised evidence-organization service that the team can actually deliver. Do not sell legal assurance, verified identity, automated debt collection, dispute prevention, tax compliance or autonomous invoicing. Validate the production product separately before offering it.
